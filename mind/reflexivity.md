## Reflexivity

Reflexivity, as I use the word, is an agent's ability to observe, judge, and change its own states, processes, or roles. It is more than self-awareness. It is leverage over how beliefs, feelings, and identities get formed and kept. It rests on four abilities: treating one's own states as things to examine, creating a pause between trigger and response, seeing that other options exist, and changing the process rather than only watching it. It runs on a scale, and it can be strengthened but never perfected.

### The Reflective Mind

The scale can be measured. [Keith Stanovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Stanovich) splits [slow thinking](two-systems.md) in two. The algorithmic mind is what intelligence tests measure. The reflective mind decides when to override the fast answer. In his words, it "determines how rational a person is." It is separate from intelligence, which explains how a very intelligent person can be unreflective. It shows in traits that cluster: a [need for cognition](three-realms.md), [active open-mindedness](trust-in-truth.md), low dogmatism, and a low need for closure. At the other end, people [seize and freeze](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8637961/) on the first answer.

### The Reflexivity Limitation

Humans face a limit I call the reflexivity limitation. It is not a failure of intelligence. It is a side effect of a mind that evolved to defend beliefs, identities, and group ties rather than revise them. Unity and quick decisions kept our ancestors alive, while open self-doubt cost status and trust. So most reasoning runs in a defensive mode. People confuse first impressions with facts and feelings with evidence. Treating one's beliefs as provisional is costly and historically rare. It works for more than a few only where institutions and material security lower the cost of being wrong. Since we do not understand [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness) well enough to redesign the mind, outside help is the realistic path.

### Augmenting Our Reflexivity

[Those who would gain most from reflexivity are the least open to it.](dunning-kruger.md) So improvement rarely comes from insight alone. It comes from institutions that change the costs and benefits of being wrong. Education, professional training, and communities of practice force encounters with opposing views. They set standards for justifying claims. They make self-correction an expectation rather than a personal failing. Where admitting error carries less shame, revision becomes routine. Institutions can supply its parts at scale and make reflection the default, not an act of discipline. The concrete design lives in the [reflexivity curriculum](reflexivity-curriculum.md).
