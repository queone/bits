## Consciousness

[Steven Pinker](pinker.md)'s [psychology lectures](https://stevenpinker.com/psy-1-introduction-psychological-science-lectures) split consciousness, as David Chalmers did, into easy problems and a [hard problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_problem_of_consciousness).

The easy problem is access. Some information is available to us: the objects we see, our plans, the content of a sentence. Some is not: the rules of syntax or the commands to our muscles.

"Easy" is an in-joke. It is still very hard, but science has a way in. [Attention](attention.md) decides what gets through. The leading account is a [global workspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_workspace_theory), a blackboard where a relevant subset of information is posted for memory, language, and planning to share.

The hard problem is sentience: why any of this feels like something. Philosophers call the raw feels [qualia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualia), such as the smell of cinnamon. Could a program feel? Could there be a [zombie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_zombie) that acts like us with nobody home?

The lectures list the answers. Dualism leaves too many open questions. Information-processing theories cannot say why a flow of bits should feel like anything, and slide toward [panpsychism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panpsychism), with conscious thermostats. Neural theories point to brain rhythms, [Penrose](penrose.md) to quantum gravity, and both still owe us the how. The [mysterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_mysterianism) say our brains may lack the means to solve it. And [Daniel Dennett](dennett.md) says there is no hard problem: sentience over and above the machinery is an illusion.

I side with Dennett. I expect a materialist explanation in the end. Life looked like this kind of mystery in the nineteenth century and turned out to be molecules organized by natural selection. Descartes's objection, that I cannot be wrong about my own experience, does not move me.

That does not make the mysterians wrong today. [Chomsky's limits](chomsky.md) on human understanding are real, I think, for the brain as it now is. They hold until we augment it, which may never happen.
