## Complexity

Terms for talking about complexity, with how settled each one is. Other entries build on these.

### Two fields share one word

- **Computational complexity** sorts problems by the time and memory it takes to solve them. Its best-known open question is [P versus NP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P_versus_NP_problem). The field is [settled mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_complexity_theory) with famous unsolved problems.
- **Complex systems** studies many interacting parts that produce behavior none of them has alone. It is an [interdisciplinary field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_system) that borrows tools from physics, biology, mathematics, and the social sciences, not a single theory. The [Santa Fe Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Institute) is its best-known home.

Popular writing slides between the two. Check which one a claim means.

### Common traps

- **Calling anything complicated emergent.** Emergence names properties the parts lack, not complication.
- **Reading chaos as disorder.** Chaotic systems follow exact rules. They are unpredictable, not random.
- **Seeing a power law in any straight line on a log plot.** [Clauset, Shalizi, and Newman](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.1062) tested published claims and found the power law ruled out in some cases. The usual least-squares fit gives inaccurate estimates.
- **Mixing the two fields.** A problem that is hard to compute is not a complex system.
- **Using the word as an explanation.** "Complexity" can do what "quantum" does in pseudoscience: sound like a reason without being one. [Detecting Baloney](detecting-baloney.md) has the checks.
