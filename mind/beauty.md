## Beauty

Our sense of [beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty) strikes me as the most redeeming thing about us. We stop to look at a sunset that gives us nothing. Where that sense comes from is an open question.

One answer is mate choice, Darwin's idea that animals choose mates for their looks, revived by [Richard Prum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Evolution_of_Beauty). A second is the useful signal: what looks good once helped us, as when children from many nations [prefer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_aesthetics) pictures of savanna with trees. A third is the by-product. [Steven Pinker](pinker.md) called music "[auditory cheesecake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_musicology)": a taste for fat and sugar was useful, and cheesecake just presses the buttons. A fourth says beauty is real: [David Deutsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Deutsch) argues for [objective beauty](https://www.nature.com/articles/526S16a), some of it universal, "[as objective as the laws of physics](https://www.onfiction.ca/2012/04/david-deutsch-on-objective-beauty.html)". His case is the flower, evolved to attract insects, which attracts us too.

I lean toward the by-product, and I get there from the body. Pleasure is the useful signal: eating when hungry feels good, and so does relieving yourself. What helps the body feels right. My guess is that beauty is the same machinery, firing at things it was never built for: a sunset, a fugue, an equation. Pleasure and beauty still differ, though. [Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critique_of_Judgment) drew the line: the agreeable is a good steak, and the beautiful is what we stop to contemplate, wanting nothing from it.

So I part with Deutsch. He thinks the standard is out there. I think it is in us, a by-product that works as a compass, drawing us to objects and ideas in harmony with what we are. That is why I call it redeeming: it is a way of choosing that owes [nothing to winning](../life/legacy.md).

[Science](science.md) tells me what is true. Beauty helps me choose what is worth wanting.
