## Attention

Findings on attention from [Steven Pinker](pinker.md)'s introductory [psychology lectures](https://stevenpinker.com/psy-1-introduction-psychological-science-lectures). Other entries build on these.

### Two ways the mind processes

- **Parallel**: many things at once. The brain handles [incoming stimuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_processing_(psychology)) of different kinds at the same time, such as color, motion, shape, and depth.
- **Serial**: one thing at a time. Whatever needs focal attention goes through this bottleneck.

### Feature integration theory

[Anne Treisman and Garry Gelade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_integration_theory), 1980.

- Single features are registered "early, automatically, and in parallel".
- A target that differs by one feature [pops out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_search), however many distractors surround it. Think of a red dot among green ones.
- A target defined by a conjunction of features does not pop out. Wikipedia's example is a green X among purple Xs and green Os. Joining the features takes focal attention, which is serial, so the search slows as the crowd grows.

### Attention and awareness

- In the lectures, attention is a necessary condition for consciousness.
- [Inattentional blindness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inattentional_blindness) is the proof. In a famous study, people busy with a visual task failed to notice a person in a gorilla costume walking through the scene.

### The global workspace

[Bernard Baars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_workspace_theory), 1988.

- Consciousness works like a blackboard. Baars took the idea from early artificial-intelligence systems in which independent programs shared information on one.
- Only a small, relevant subset of information is posted.
- What is posted is shared among mid-level perception, language, memory, and planning.
- In the lectures, the brain side of this is connectivity to the frontal lobes.

What reaches the blackboard, and what never does, is covered under [Consciousness](consciousness.md).
