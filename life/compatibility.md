## Compatibility

I think romantic compatibility runs on the same [three realms](../mind/three-realms.md) as the rest of life: the physical, the emotional, and the intellectual. A lasting match needs fit in all three, though not a perfect one. In love, fit in each realm is a matter of taste and feeling. Robert Sternberg's [triangular theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_theory_of_love), the best-known model of love, also has three parts: intimacy, passion, and commitment. It leaves out the intellect, and it leaves out luck.

The order of the realms matters a little too. Partners who rank the realms the same way may get along more easily. The similarity evidence says not to lean on that. A [meta-analysis of 313 studies](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0265407508096700) found that actual similarity stopped predicting attraction once people were in a relationship. Perceived similarity still predicted it.

The fourth factor is serendipity, the luck that brings two people together. It is not a realm, because it is not something a mind does. The three realms describe the fit, but luck decides whether two people meet at all, and when. Plain nearness shapes who pairs up, which psychologists call the [propinquity effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propinquity). Fit is also hard to foresee. In a [2017 speed-dating study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28853645/), over 100 measures of traits and preferences could not predict which specific pairs would want each other.

So I see the three realms as the part of compatibility we can judge once we meet, and luck as the part we cannot. Luck is partly built, as [Personal Growth](personal-growth.md) argues. It still gets a vote.
