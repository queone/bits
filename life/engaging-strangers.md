## Engaging Strangers

Most of us would rather not talk to strangers, and most of us are wrong about it. Commuters asked to talk with a stranger [enjoyed the ride more](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25019381/) than those who kept to themselves, though people expected the opposite. After a conversation, people [underestimate how much the other person liked them](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30183512/). The steps below lower the cost of the first move for both sides.

1. **Collapse anonymity fast.** Make brief eye contact and give a small smile or an [eyebrow flash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyebrow_flash). Stand slightly angled, not squared up, so the approach does not read as a challenge.
2. **Introduce a shared frame.** Make an observation about something you can both see, not an opinion. No questions and nothing personal yet. Simply narrate reality.
3. **Lower the response burden.** Give them several off-ramps. Paradoxically, reminding people they are free to say no [tends to raise](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/download/2640/3402) the chance they say yes.
4. **Signal an easy exit.** Keep your feet and body pointed toward what you were already doing.
5. **Escalate only after reciprocity.** No signal, no escalation. Once they respond, a question is welcome, and a [follow-up question](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28447835/) makes people like you more.
6. **Mirror once.** If they agree briefly, echo it: "Yeah. Brutal."
7. **Go further only if they add something of their own.** A short answer is a polite close. Take it.
