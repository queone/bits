## The Money Exception

In 2011 [Sam Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Harris) found the one thing his readers would not forgive. He could call religions "cesspools of confusion and bigotry", deny free will, even defend torture, he wrote, and keep his audience. Then he [suggested](https://www.samharris.org/blog/how-rich-is-too-rich) billionaires pay more tax. "Nothing will rile and winnow your audience" like that, he [reported afterward](https://www.samharris.org/blog/how-to-lose-readers-without-even-trying), calling the reaction a "quasi-religious abhorrence of 'wealth redistribution'".

I see a quieter version among thinkers I admire. [Steven Pinker](../mind/pinker.md) argues in [Enlightenment Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightenment_Now) that economic inequality "is not itself a dimension of human wellbeing". That is a position, not a motive. This is only my impression: remedies that touch big money face a higher bar of proof among these thinkers.

Some of that bar is sound. [Chesterton's fence](../mind/famous-lessons.md) says not to tear down what you do not understand, and rocking a working economy can make things far worse. Some of it is ordinary bias. Psychologists call the pull toward things as they are [status quo bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo_bias), and the urge to defend existing arrangements [system justification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_justification).

I cannot draw the exact line between the two, but timing matters. In February 1933, [25 German industrialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Meeting_of_20_February_1933) met Hitler and gave about two million Reichsmarks to his campaign. I wonder how that year would have gone if more people like them had said no. [Dwight Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower%27s_farewell_address), a general and no radical, spoke up on his way out, warning against "unwarranted influence" by the "military-industrial complex".

So those who respect the fence still ought to speak louder when the moment looks consequential. That asks thinkers to read a crystal ball, perhaps unfairly. But I agree with [Chomsky](../mind/chomsky.md) that [intellectuals](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Noam_Chomsky) "are in a position to expose the lies of governments", and that position carries a duty. I would only ask for more diplomacy than he shows.
