## Virtue Signaling

[Virtue signaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtue_signalling) is a form of hypocrisy: projecting moral values for social approval while avoiding real-world action. A post, a slogan, or a symbol costs little. Doing something about the cause costs time, money, or standing.

My twist is that it is often unintentional. Good intentions get mixed with hidden self-interest, or with a little subconscious narcissism, and the person doing it rarely notices. That is why it is so common. Most of us would rather feel good about a stance than pay for it.

The charge itself deserves care. [Critics of the term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtue_signalling) point out that accusing someone of virtue signaling can be its own kind of signaling, and research finds that people accused of it often feel genuine outrage. Calling a view virtue signaling dismisses the speaker without answering the view, the mistake known as [Bulverism](../mind/famous-lessons.md#bulverism).

So I use one test, on others and on myself: what did the stance cost? A moral view that comes with action, money, or risk is not signaling, whatever its tone. One that comes with none may be sincere, but it has not yet done anything.
