## Social Roles

A cheat-sheet of the roles people hold in society, kept for other entries to point to. In [role theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role_theory), a role is the set of expectations a society attaches to a position, not a trait of the person. The theory maps the slots, not the people in them. What pulls a person toward a slot, and who ends up filling it, is partly evolved and partly temperament, as [Tooby](../mind/tooby.md) and [Domain-Specific Models](../mind/domain-specific-models.md) cover. Everyone holds several at once. The five domains below are my own grouping of the standard list, and the refinements are the field's own terms.

### Five domains

Each domain is defined by the institution that hands out the role and enforces it.

| Domain | Roles | How assigned | Who enforces |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kin | child, sibling, partner, parent, elder | at birth, later partly by choice | the family |
| Work | worker, provider, apprentice, colleague, boss, professional | by effort and choice | the employer and the trade |
| Civic | citizen, neighbor, taxpayer, voter, juror, soldier | by birthplace or residence | the state |
| Associational | friend, member, congregant, volunteer, fan, regular | by choice, and freely left | the group, through loyalty only |
| Transactional | customer, client, patient, student, guest, passenger | for the length of the exchange | the counterpart |

- A kin role can last a lifetime, and it is the first one anybody holds.
- A work role is the one that answers "what do you do?" in a market society.
- A civic role carries duties the state defines and can compel.
- An associational role carries the weakest sanctions, so it rests on loyalty.
- A transactional role is always paired with its complement: seller, provider, doctor, teacher, host, driver.

### Refinements

- **Ascribed versus achieved.** [Ralph Linton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Linton) drew the line in 1936. An [ascribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascribed_status) role comes from birth or circumstance: sex, age, kin, nationality. An [achieved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achieved_status) role comes from effort or choice: occupation, membership, parenthood. Most roles mix both over a life.
- **Roles come in pairs.** There is no parent without a child, no teacher without a student, no seller without a buyer. A role is a relationship, not a trait, though temperament steers who takes which role. That is the main difference from the mythic [archetypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungian_archetypes): a Warrior stands alone, but a soldier needs a state.
- **Role set.** [Robert K. Merton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_K._Merton)'s term for the cluster of relationships one position carries. A teacher deals with students, parents, colleagues, administrators, and the school board, each with its own expectations.
- **Master status.** The one role that overrides the rest in how others see you, a term from Everett Hughes. Doctor, celebrity, convict, and disability all work as a [master status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_status). Race and gender often act as one whether the person wants that or not.
- **Role conflict versus role strain.** [Conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role_conflict) is between two roles, such as parent and worker pulling in opposite directions. Strain is inside one role, such as a manager who must both support and evaluate the same person.
- **Life-course roles.** Child, student, worker, parent, retiree, elder. Societies mark each transition with a [rite of passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rite_of_passage). The transition is where the old script stops working and the new one is not yet learned.
- **Gender roles.** A cross-cutting ascribed layer that changes the expected script inside every other domain. Wikipedia covers the research under [gender role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_role).
- **Front stage and back stage.** [Erving Goffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erving_Goffman) described roles as [performances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramaturgy_(sociology)) for an audience, dropped in private. Role distance is the visible signal that someone is playing a role without owning it.
- **Deviant and marginal roles.** [Simmel's stranger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stranger_(sociology)) is near the group but not of it. [Talcott Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talcott_Parsons)'s [sick role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sick_role) excuses the sick person from normal duties and obliges them to seek help and try to recover. [Labeling theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labeling_theory) adds that being called deviant is itself a role assignment, and one that tends to stick.

### Mythic roles versus social roles

The Sage, the Warrior, and the Peacemaker of myth map onto social roles, but loosely. The Sage is the teacher or expert, a work role. The Warrior is the soldier or police officer, a civic role. Peacemaking is usually a function inside another role rather than a role of its own: elders, parents, judges, and diplomats all do it. The standalone version appears only when a society formalizes it, as [mediator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediation) or [ombudsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ombudsman). The oldest mythic set, sovereign and priest, warrior, and producer, is the [trifunctional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trifunctional_hypothesis) division Georges Dumézil found across Indo-European societies.

A role is held, not owned. [Reflexivity](../mind/reflexivity.md) is the ability to see and change one's own roles, and [Hold Your Identity Lightly](../mind/identity-lightly.md) makes the same case for labels.
