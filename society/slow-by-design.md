## Slow by Design

[Jonathan Haidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Haidt) wrote in a [2022 essay](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2022/05/social-media-democracy-trust-babel/629369/) that the Framers "were excellent social psychologists." A delegate's [notes](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Madison/01-10-02-0044) record [James Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison) telling the Convention in 1787 that a people may want checks "against the turbulency and weakness of unruly passions." They built a republic that is slow on purpose, designed, Haidt writes, to "slow things down, cool passions, require compromise." The features are under [Constitutional Republic](constitutional-republic.md).

Haidt's claim is that social media stripped the cooling away when platforms added the tools of virality, from 2009 to 2012. Anger now outruns institutions. The mechanics are under [Viral Truth](viral-truth.md), [The Perception Gap](perception-gap.md), and [Perception Management](perception-management.md).

I agree with Haidt, though the evidence is mixed and [we may both be wrong](../mind/popper.md). A [2017 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28928150/) found polarization grew most among Americans over 65, who use social media least. Haidt's [reply](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2022/07/social-media-harm-facebook-meta-response/670975/) is that his claim concerns a sudden shift in the early 2010s, which long trend lines cannot show. I have no full answer to that yet.

Haidt names three reforms: harden democratic institutions, reform social media, and prepare the next generation. I back all three and would add two. The first he practices without listing it: speaking out calmly, with evidence, builds the critical mass change needs. That is the [duty to speak](money-exception.md), with [civility](smug-pilots.md).

Slowness served the republic well, and so far we have managed to "[keep it](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin)". But it cannot be the whole answer as technology speeds everything up. My second addition is AI, which I think can help us [chill out](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2023/04/ai-to-aid-democracy.html). It could check facts at the speed of virality. It could tell me what Haidt, [Pinker](../mind/pinker.md), [Popper](../mind/popper.md), [Chomsky](../mind/chomsky.md), or [Rauch](../mind/trust-in-truth.md) would say about a thought of mine, so I could admit error sooner. That depends on AI being [aligned](../tech/before-agi.md) with us and earning our trust.
