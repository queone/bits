## Oligarchy

An [oligarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligarchy) is rule by a few, and in practice by the few who are rich. It is tempting to pin the state of U.S. politics on [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump) alone. In my reading he is the face, and the lasting force is concentrated wealth that shapes the law. The test is to look at who gains and who pays.

Take the [tax and spending law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Big_Beautiful_Bill_Act) he signed in July 2025. The [Congressional Budget Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Budget_Office) (CBO), the nonpartisan scorekeeper of Congress, [estimates](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61461) that it adds $3.4 trillion to deficits through 2034, or [$4.1 trillion](https://www.crfb.org/blogs/whats-one-big-beautiful-bill-act) with interest. CBO's [household analysis](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61469) of August 2025 shows where the money goes. The poorest tenth of households lose about $1,200 a year, mostly through cuts to Medicaid and food aid. The richest tenth gain about [$13,600](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/cbo-analyzes-distributional-effects-of-budget-act/) a year.

The pattern is older than that law. In his first term Trump approved [$8.4 trillion](https://www.crfb.org/papers/trump-and-biden-national-debt) of new ten-year borrowing, against $4.3 trillion under Joe Biden through mid-2024. Most of it was bipartisan, which is part of the point. The largest partisan item was the [2017 tax cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_Cuts_and_Jobs_Act), at $1.9 trillion.

Now follow the campaign money. One hundred billionaire families spent [$2.6 billion](https://americansfortaxfairness.org/billionaires-buying-elections-theyve-come-to-collect/) on the 2024 federal elections, one dollar in every six. [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk) alone spent more than [$290 million](https://www.cnn.com/2025/02/01/politics/elon-musk-2024-election-spending-millions), then became the de facto leader of the new administration's [cost-cutting office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency). Yet Forbes counted [more billionaires](https://fortune.com/2024/10/23/billioniares-back-harris-more-than-trump-musk-cuban/) behind Kamala Harris than behind Trump, 81 to 51. The money sits on both sides, which is why removing one man would not fix it. Biden himself [warned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden%27s_farewell_address) on leaving office that an oligarchy was taking hold.

The [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/dataviz/dfa/distribute/table/) shows the result. By mid-2026 the top 1% held 32.5% of the nation's household wealth, and the bottom half held 2.3%.

Supporters answer that growth and tariffs cover the cost. CBO's growth-adjusted estimate came out [higher](https://www.crfb.org/blogs/obbba-dynamic-score-comes-47-trillion), not lower, because more borrowing raises interest rates. The Supreme Court [struck down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_Resources,_Inc._v._Trump) the emergency tariffs in February 2026, and CBO now projects a [$2.1 trillion](https://fortune.com/2026/08/10/how-big-deficit-national-debt-tariffs-cbo/) deficit for fiscal 2026.

Whether "oligarchy" is the right word is still [argued](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2053168015608896) among political scientists. The ledger is not. [Identity](us-identity-politics.md) sells the story, and money writes the law. [Markets](markets-capitalism.md) are tools, so the remedy is [rules](rule-of-law.md), not a change of face.
