## Politics

The classic poles of politics were named by [Thomas Sowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Sowell) in [A Conflict of Visions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Conflict_of_Visions) as the constrained and unconstrained visions. [Steven Pinker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Pinker) reworked them in [The Blank Slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blank_Slate) as the tragic and utopian visions. They are the right and left we all argue about, and I am no exception.

I would rename the poles **Reason** and **Compassion**. Reason should guide us to a just and effective government. It should always be tempered by an understanding of human nature and by empathy in governing. The prudent stance is a hybrid, with the moment in history deciding what is practical.

[Sowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Sowell)'s account seems largely right but partial to his libertarian reading of the constrained vision. I disagree that either vision is as likely to cause harm as the other. The harm each brings is particular to history, not to the vision. That opinion may be premature.

Many self-described centrists find conservatives hard to talk to because the conservative pole has moved to the right. The centrist position is reason plus compassion, leaning slightly left, and "balance" captures it.

Three smaller convictions. Vote for the politicians whose views and policies most closely match your own. The family and social class you are born into is sheer luck, the [lottery of birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lottery_of_birth), and it pays to remember it. And beware blinding, hateful feelings. Such feelings have fueled countless atrocities, and the dark side is more real than we think.
