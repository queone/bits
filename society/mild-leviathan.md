## Mild Leviathan

[Hobbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leviathan_%28Hobbes_book%29) thought people would trade freedom for safety and hand a sovereign the sword. The [state of nature](../mind/core-challenges.md) he feared is real enough. But his cure was a monster, and every state since has had to be watched. I lean toward something milder, though I am not sure what.

[Elinor Ostrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elinor_Ostrom) showed that people who share a resource can write their own rules and keep them, without a sovereign or a sale. [Thaler and Sunstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_paternalism) showed that a nudge can steer a choice while leaving the exit open. Both are mild Leviathans. The group gets the [slow thinking](../mind/two-systems.md) that no single member supplies, and consent stays with the member. That answers the [libertarian](libertarianism.md) objection, since nobody is forced.

What I add is speculative, and may be utopian. The [reflexivity](../mind/reflexivity.md) we lack could be supplied by an AI we contract with, one at a time, on the honor system. It would hold the pause, name the bias, and recall what we said last year. Self-governance assisted that way might work where slow institutions [cannot keep up](slow-by-design.md). The condition is an AI that is [aligned](../tech/before-agi.md) and has earned our trust, which is [Stuart Russell's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Compatible) whole problem.

My reading is that this is what separates the AI optimists from the [doomsayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_risk_from_artificial_intelligence). Both see a Leviathan coming. The optimist thinks it can be kept mild, and volunteered. The doomsayer thinks no Leviathan stays mild. I am with the optimists for now, and every claim here has a sell-by date.
